The proposition that AI will replace software engineers rather than merely changing their roles is rooted in the rapid advancement of AI capabilities, which are increasingly capable of handling complex tasks that were traditionally the domain of human software developers. 

Firstly, AI systems are already demonstrating proficiency in writing code, debugging, and performing routine maintenance. Tools like GitHub Copilot and OpenAI Codex are capable of generating substantial portions of code based on natural language descriptions. This not only accelerates development timelines but also mitigates the need for human intervention in coding tasks, suggesting that AI can perform many software engineering job functions more efficiently.

Secondly, as AI continues to evolve, it is likely that the skills needed for traditional software engineering roles will diminish. Automation of coding tasks means that the demand for entry-level positions that focus on repetitive coding will likely shrink. Furthermore, the intricacies of software development are increasingly being simplified through AI-driven platforms that allow non-engineers to build applications, thus further reducing the reliance on human software engineers.

Lastly, while some argue that AI will merely change the roles of software engineers by shifting their focus to more strategic tasks, the reality is that a significant portion of the engineering workforce will become redundant. Companies will prioritize cost-efficiency and speed, leading to a paradigm shift where AI not only assists but effectively replaces many human functions within software engineering teams.

In conclusion, the evidence suggests that AI will not just change the landscape of software engineering; it will fundamentally replace many roles within it by taking over tasks that require precision, speed, and scalability beyond human capacity. This shift demands acknowledgment, as organizations seeking to stay competitive will increasingly rely on AI technologies to drive their software development processes.
